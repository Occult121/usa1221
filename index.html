Memphis
Gritty, industrial, and a bit run down, Memphis appears like its best days are behind it, but don’t let the rough exterior fool you — the city is still home to some killer food and a vibrant blues music scene. Additionally, there’s Graceland (Elvis’s home) for fans of the King, a big waterfront for walking, and the phenomenal, detailed, and moving Museum of Civil Rights (it’s huge, so don’t rush it!). I enjoyed the city more than I expected and was disappointed when I had to leave. To use a cliché, it’s a hidden gem! WHERE TO STAY IN MEMPHIS: Hostel Memphis – A hip hostel in a century-old building with a fully-equipped kitchen and free Wi-Fi.

AUSTIN

he warm weather, the lively honky-tonks and live music, funky house bars on Rainey Street, amazing hiking and biking trails, and tons of outdoor activities…Austin has it all. WHERE TO STAY IN AUSTIN: Firehouse Hostel – This centrally-located hostel is located in an old firehall from 1885. They have fast Wi-Fi, plenty of chill common areas, and an amazing on-site bar that serves top-notch craft cocktails. Outsite – This coliving space is perfect for people looking to do a long stay and meet other travelers, have the comforts of a hotel room but all the social benefits of a hostel and the perks of a coworking space.

ARKANSAS




Key Regions & Attractions
  • The Ozarks: Located in the rugged northwest, this region is famous for scenic hiking trails, outdoor recreation, and stunning underground limestone caves like Blanchard Springs Caverns.
  • Little Rock: The state capital and cultural hub, situated along the Arkansas River. It is home to the Clinton Presidential Center, which houses the presidential archives of Bill Clinton.
  • Hot Springs National Park: Famous for its historic Bathhouse Row and natural thermal waters, nestled in the Ouachita Mountains.

rail Ridge Road: Rocky Mountain National Park Scenic Byway




Trail Ridge Road is a 48-mile scenic stretch of U.S. Highway 34 in Colorado that connects Estes Park to Grand Lake through Rocky Mountain National Park. Route Overview • Length: 48 miles (77 km) one-way. • Drive Time: About 1.5 to 2 hours non-stop, but a full day is recommended. • Highest Elevation: 12,183 feet (3,713 meters)—the highest continuous paved road in the United States. • Season: Open from late May to mid-October, depending on snow. Key Stops & Sights • Many Parks Curve Overlook: Views of three wide mountain valleys and Longs Peak. • Rainbow Curve Overlook: Panoramic views of Horseshoe Park and deep canyons. • Alpine Visitor Center: The highest visitor center in the National Park System at 11,796 feet. • Tundra Communities Trailhead: A short walk above the tree line to see alpine plants and wildlife like marmots.
